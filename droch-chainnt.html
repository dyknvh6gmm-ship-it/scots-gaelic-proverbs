Droch-chainnt sa Ghàidhlig 18+

Swearing, insults and near-the-knuckle Gaelic — registers, curses, and a bit of the language nobody teaches you in class.

⚠️ Mature language content

This page is for adults. It covers swearing, insults, and explicit vocabulary in Scottish Gaelic — presented for its genuine linguistic and cultural value, the same way a dictionary of profanity in any language would be. If that's not what you're after, no bother — the Grammar and Resources pages are right next door.

Registers of politeness are marked throughout, but use your own judgement: none of this will land the same everywhere, especially with older folk in rural areas. What you do with any of it is on you.

Swearing and politeness both depend on the context you're in. It isn't polite to say "Hey, pal!" to the King, but it's perfectly fine with a friend, or even a stranger, down the pub. In the same way, it's quite impolite to say "Jesus!" in a church or among strongly religious people — but not in most other settings.

In Gaelic specifically, because there hasn't been a Gaelic-speaking nobility for over 200 years, and there aren't many ministers or priests with Gaelic left either, the highest registers of the language are fading. The lowest registers are disappearing too, in much the same way: swearing is normally picked up from peers, not taught by parents or at school. Because Gaelic isn't much used among young people any more, and now feels natural only in specific settings — church, school, semi-formal community events — there's little chance left to socialise in the kind of casual, unguarded company where swearing gets learned. Historically, women would swear (or at least talk more freely) while washing or waulking cloth together, and men while out on the hill or at sea — informal, friendly company, in both cases.

What counts as swearing at all is also a matter of cultural heritage. Gaelic tradition is closely tied to Christianity, so swearing that invokes religion carries noticeably more weight in Gaelic than the equivalent does in English.

💩 Tapagan (Swears)

Exclamations — the Gaelic equivalent of "damn it!" through to much stronger stuff.

Polite
Mo chreach-sa thàinig!My goodness! Good grief!
A chiall!Good grief! (lit. "O sense!")
M' anam!Goodness me! (lit. "My soul!")
Polite, depending on context

Religious oaths — not fine in church or with strongly religious folk, but safe enough generally.

An ainm an Àigh!For goodness' sake! (lit. "in the name of the Blessed one")
Impolite
Cac!Shit!
Daing!Damn!
Dògan!Blast!
A Dhiabhail!Damn it! (lit. "the Devil!")

Droch abairtean eile (Other bad expressions)

Everyday frustration and emphasis — the Gaelic "bloody" and "fucking".

Strong, without being too rude
Dè fon ghrèin a tha seo?What on Earth is this?
Cò fon ghrèin a th' annaibh?Who the heck are you?
Chuir e thuige mi!It really annoyed me.
Bha mi diombach dheth!I was mad at it.
Chàirich thu gu h-olc e!You screwed it up.
Rinn thu bùrach dheth!You made a mess of it.
Chan eil ann ach drafas/truilleas/sgudal!It's a whole lot of rubbish!
Impolite
Tha mi air mo lionnrachadh leis!I'm really pissed off at him.
Rinn thu tòn dheth!You fucked it up.
Chan eil spùt de thuigse aige!He has no bloody clue!
Bugair"bloody" — an intensifier
'S e bugair bùraich a th' ann!It's a bloody mess!
Tha an donas coimpiutair briste a-rithistThe bloody computer's broken again!
Mo thòn a bhith am Beàrnaraigh!What bullshit! (lit. "my arse in Bernera!")
Very impolite

Invoking the Devil's name is far stronger in Gaelic than English's fairly mild "the devil" — treat it like the F-word.

Dè an diabhal a tha seo?What the fuck is this?
Cò an diabhal a th' annaibh?Who the fuck are you?
Cho cinnteach is a tha bod san eachSure as fuck
Chan eil diù a' choin agam dha!I don't give a fuck about it!
Diabhalta, diabhlaidh"fucking" — a strong intensifier
Abair bùrach diabhalta a tha seo!What a fucking mess!
Tha mi diabhlaidh coma dheth!I don't give a fuck about it!
Gabh an còta diabhalta agad agus Hiort ort!Take your fucking coat and bugger off!
An donachd càil a gheibh thu bhuaithe!You won't get a damn thing from him!
Tha fios agad diabhlaidh math!You know bloody well!

Facail ceangailte le feis (Sex-related words)

Words used neutrally for animals (mating, breeding) are always rude when applied to people — that shift matters more in Gaelic than it does in English.

  • Bigein — genitalia (general)
  • Clachan — testicles (neutral)
  • Drabasta — lewd, obscene
  • Drabastachd — obscene/lewd material
